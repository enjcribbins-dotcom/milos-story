# The First Flight

By the next morning, Ember had discovered that the cabin was full of interesting things.

The table was interesting. Black Paw's tail was interesting. Milo's boots were extremely interesting.

But most of all, the open window was interesting.

Ember climbed onto the sill.

Milo hurried over. “Wait.”

Ember looked back.

“You don't know how to fly.”

The little dragon spread his wings.

Milo sighed. “That doesn't mean you know how.”

Ember jumped.

For one terrifying second he dropped.

Then his wings snapped open.

He flapped once.

Twice.

He rose.

Milo stared.

“You're flying!”

Ember made a delighted chirping sound and circled the cabin.

Milo ran outside.

“Come on!”

Ember followed.

The morning air rushed around them as the dragon climbed higher.

Milo ran until he reached the edge of a small stream.

He stopped beside the water, breathing hard.

Ember hovered above him.

Milo laughed. “I wish I could do that.”

The thought came without warning.

He looked at the water.

A small ribbon lifted from the stream.

Milo froze.

He had not meant to use his power.

The ribbon twisted through the air.

Then it fell.

Milo dropped to one knee.

Black Paw was beside him immediately.

“I’m fine.”

He was not entirely fine.

His arms felt heavy.

But something else had changed.

Ember landed beside him.

Milo reached out.

“You make it look easy.”

Ember nudged his hand.

Milo smiled.

“Maybe you'll teach me.”

The dragon chirped.

For the rest of the morning, Ember practised.

Milo ran beside him.

Black Paw followed.

They had become a team without ever deciding to.

That afternoon, Ember carried Milo a short distance above the clearing.

Milo gripped the dragon carefully.

The ground dropped away.

For one breath, Milo was afraid.

Then he looked down.

The forest spread beneath them.

The mountains rose beyond it.

And for the first time in his life, Milo saw the valley from the sky.

“It’s beautiful,” he whispered.

Ember turned his head.

Milo laughed.

“Again.”

Far beyond the trees, something answered with a distant roar.
