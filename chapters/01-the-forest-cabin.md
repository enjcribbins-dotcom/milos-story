# The Forest Cabin

Milo lived in a valley surrounded by snowy mountains.

From the window of the little cabin, he could see the forest beginning just beyond the clearing. In summer the trees were deep green. In autumn they turned gold. In winter the whole valley disappeared beneath snow.

The cabin was home, but the forest was where Milo felt most himself.

He had grown up among those trees with Black Paw beside him.

Black Paw was a wolf—not a person, not a storybook creature wearing armour, but a real wolf with sharp ears, a powerful body, and a habit of appearing whenever Milo needed him. They had known each other for as long as Milo could remember.

Milo was eleven now.

He was curious about everything.

That morning, he stood beside the cabin and held out one hand toward a patch of grass. A few blades bent toward his fingers.

“Come on,” Milo whispered.

The grass moved.

Then it stopped.

Milo frowned and tried again.

A small green shoot lifted from the earth.

For a moment he smiled.

Then his arm began to ache.

Milo let his hand fall.

Black Paw watched him from the edge of the clearing.

“I know,” Milo said. “I used too much.”

Black Paw gave a quiet huff.

Milo laughed. “You don't have to say it.”

He knew the rules, even if he did not understand where his strange abilities came from.

Plants listened to him—but only plants close enough to reach. Water was different. If there was no water nearby, there was nothing for him to control.

And whenever he pushed himself too hard, the strength drained out of him.

Sometimes Milo wondered whether his parents had known about these powers.

He remembered very little about them. They had been adventurers, Black Paw had told him. They had travelled beyond the forest and searched for things most people believed were only legends.

Then, when Milo was about two years old, they never came home.

Black Paw had stayed.

The forest had become Milo's home.

And there were still things Black Paw would not tell him.

Milo looked toward the trees.

“Do you think they knew what I could do?”

Black Paw looked away.

Milo sighed. “That's what I thought.”

He grabbed his small pack and headed for the forest.

Black Paw followed.

The morning was quiet. Birds moved through the branches. Leaves whispered overhead. Milo knew nearly every path around the cabin.

But that day something felt different.

They had gone only a short distance when Black Paw stopped.

His ears rose.

Milo stopped too.

“What is it?”

Black Paw stared at the ground.

Milo crouched beside him.

There, pressed into the soft earth beneath the trees, was a track.

It was far too large to belong to any ordinary animal Milo knew.

He looked at Black Paw.

Black Paw looked deeper into the forest.

Then he started walking.

Milo followed.

Neither of them knew it yet, but the tracks would lead them toward a secret that would change everything.
